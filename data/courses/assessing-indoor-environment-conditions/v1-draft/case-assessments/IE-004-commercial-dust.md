## 1. Case card

Toowoomba, Queensland, March 2027. A single-storey 1978 shopping strip. A medical-centre tenancy
is being refitted while the pharmacy next door keeps trading. The tenancies share one ceiling void
and one ducted air-conditioning return. The property manager engaged you after pharmacy staff
reported fine grey dust on shelves and sore eyes: *Where is the dust coming from, and what must
happen to stop it?* On the walk-round from the public side of the hoarding you see:

- shopfitters cutting fibre-cement wall lining with a circular saw; the wall lining measures
  3.0 m × 4.8 m = 14.4 m² (IEA-205); no asbestos register on site;
- an electrician chasing a brick wall with an angle grinder, no dust extraction;
- a painter dry-sanding original painted timber window frames;
- a carpenter cutting softwood framing with a drop saw;
- a labourer mixing Portland cement for a slab patch;
- a diesel forklift unloading in the rear loading dock, doors open to the tenancy;
- the hoarding stops at the ceiling grid; the void above it is open for 6.0 m × 0.3 m = 1.8 m².

## 3. Reasoned Five Whys

1. **Why is there dust on the pharmacy shelves?** *Evidence:* film heaviest on the shared-wall side;
   counts rise while the grinder runs and fall when it stops; outdoor and unaffected-tenancy counts
   are steady and similar. The street is not the main source. A particle counter is an indicator: it
   shows a pathway, not what the dust is (IEA-203).
2. **Why does it reach the pharmacy?** The hoarding stops at the ceiling grid, leaving 1.8 m² open
   into a shared void, and the tenancies share a return-air path.
3. **Why is so much dust being made?** Dry cutting, grinding and sanding with no on-tool extraction,
   local exhaust ventilation or wet methods (IEA-191).
4. **Why were these hazards not controlled before work began?** No asbestos register was on site
   for a pre-2004 building (IEA-186, IEA-202), the paint was not tested, and the work was not
   planned around silica and dust controls. These are the PCBU's duties: eliminate or minimise risk
   so far as is reasonably practicable (IEA-182), arrange air monitoring when unsure of RCS levels
   (IEA-192).
5. **Why did nobody stop it?** The site had no person checking hazardous materials before cutting.
   That is the fixable cause: the PCBU's planning and supervision.

**Stop and refer, in this order:** (1) stop the fibre-cement cutting and keep people away; refer to
a licensed asbestos assessor or competent person; if confirmed non-friable, 14.4 m² is more than
10 m², so a class B (or class A) removalist does it in Queensland (IEA-187, IEA-188); (2) stop dry
grinding and dry sanding until controls are in place; (3) the PCBU engages an occupational
hygienist for worker exposure monitoring (IEA-204; the AIOH is the Australian peak body, IEA-094);
(4) seal the ceiling void and isolate the shared return before work resumes; (5) the pharmacy is
cleaned with methods that do not re-suspend dust, and its staff are told to see a doctor about
symptoms.

## 7. Student debrief

- You stopped at suspect asbestos before anything else, and you did not take a sample yourself.
- You used the particle counter to find a **pathway**, comparing with outdoors and an unaffected
  tenancy at the same times, never with a workplace limit.
- You answered the PCBU's date questions correctly: in Queensland in 2027, softwood dust and
  Portland cement stay on the WES and DPM on the interim 0.1 mg/m³ TWA until 1 December 2029.
- You told the pharmacy manager that 0.05 mg/m³ is a workers' limit and not a clearance level for
  customers.
- **New Zealand learners:** relocate the case to Hamilton. Any building built or renovated before
  2000 could contain asbestos (IEA-199); up to and including 10 m² of non-friable asbestos over the
  whole project needs no licence, more needs a Class A or Class B removalist (IEA-198); presume
  lead-based paint in buildings from the 1980s or earlier (IEA-197); RCS WES 0.025 mg/m³ (IEA-200).

## 8. Instructor notes

- **Common errors to mark down:** collecting offcuts to "get it tested"; wetting the sheet and
  carrying on; treating 14.4 m² as unlicensed work; quoting the new national WEL for softwood dust
  or Portland cement in Queensland in 2027; "no limit" for DPM (that is flour dust); telling the
  pharmacy it is "clear" because silica is under 0.05 mg/m³; one national date for every substance.
- **Practical stations:** (1) particle counter: zero check, same channel, paired readings indoors,
  outdoors and in an unaffected space at matched times; (2) trainer demonstration: smoke-pencil
  airflow at a hoarding gap; (3) learner choice: write the stop order and the referral for each
  trigger; (4) limitations exercise: list what an optical counter cannot tell you (composition,
  asbestos, silica, lead); (5) debrief with the key below. Instrument procedures: GAP IEA-209.
- **Quiz answer key** (`5f1eb696-…`): Q1 C, Q2 B, Q3 D, Q4 A, Q5 D, Q6 A, Q7 B, Q8 C, Q9 A
  (correctIndex 2, 1, 3, 0, 3, 0, 1, 2, 0). Unsafe options that must stay wrong: "Collect the
  offcuts for lab testing", "The new WEL applied from December 2026", "No limit until 1 December
  2029" (for DPM), "Yes, below 0.05 means customers safe".

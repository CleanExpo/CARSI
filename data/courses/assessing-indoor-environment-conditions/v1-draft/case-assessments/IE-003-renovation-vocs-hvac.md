## 1. Case card

A two-storey townhouse in Melbourne, Victoria, in June. Three weeks ago the owners finished a
kitchen renovation: new particleboard and MDF cabinets with painted doors, a new high-capacity
ducted rangehood, a second bathroom exhaust fan, and draught-sealing of doors and windows. The
ducted heating return-air grille was moved to the hallway beside the kitchen, and the sink was
moved, with its floor waste capped and re-run. An open-flued gas space heater in the living room
is used most evenings. The occupants (two adults and a 14-year-old with asthma) report a
"chemical" smell in the kitchen and headaches "worse in the evenings". The owners commissioned the
visit: *What is causing the smell and the evening headaches, and what should happen next?*

Readings at 4:00 pm, heater off (illustrative):

| Reading | Value | Instrument / mode | Location |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Air temperature, RH | 21 °C, 50 %RH | Thermo-hygrometer | Kitchen, 1.2 m |
| Air temperature, RH | 14 °C, 70 %RH | Same | Outdoors, rear courtyard |
| TVOC indicator | 2.1 kitchen / 0.4 bedroom / 0.1 outdoors | PID indicator, isobutylene-equivalent ppm, zeroed outdoors | 0.5 m from new cabinets; upstairs bedroom; outdoors |
| CO | 0 ppm | Personal CO monitor, worn all visit, bump-tested that morning | Whole visit |
| Moisture (relative scale) | 11 under sink / 12 reference | Non-invasive meter | Cabinet base vs dry internal wall |

Derived (IEA-176): kitchen **7.7 g/kg** (dew point 10.2 °C); outdoors **6.9 g/kg** (dew point
8.6 °C).

## 2. Observation, measurement, hypothesis (IEA-211)

| Observation | Measurement | Hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New particleboard and MDF cabinets, painted doors, three weeks old | TVOC 2.1 kitchen vs 0.4 bedroom vs 0.1 outdoors | Product emissions, including formaldehyde, from new joinery and paint (IEA-150 to IEA-152, IEA-173) |
| "Chemical" smell; headaches reported (worse in evenings) | — | VOCs are one competing explanation for headaches (IEA-154); combustion products are another |
| Open-flued heater used with rangehood and bathroom fans; house draught-sealed | CO 0 ppm with heater **off** | Negative pressure drawing flue gases indoors when fans and heater run together (IEA-155, IEA-158) |
| Return-air grille moved beside the kitchen | — | Ducted heating spreads kitchen air through the house |
| Sink moved, floor waste re-run | Under-sink moisture 11 vs reference 12 | Dry or unsealed floor-waste trap admitting drain air (IEA-175); hidden leak (weakened by the reading) |
| Cleaning sprays and air freshener in use | — | Consumer products adding VOCs |

## 3. Reasoned Five Whys

1. **Why does the kitchen smell "chemical"?** *Evidence:* the TVOC indicator reads highest beside
   the new cabinets and falls with distance and outdoors. VOCs often have an odour and come from
   construction materials and new furnishings (IEA-150); most off-gassing happens when products are
   new (IEA-151); particleboard and MDF resins can off-gas formaldehyde for years (IEA-152, IEA-173).
   The indicator does **not** say which compounds, and it does not measure CO (IEA-174).
2. **Why are the headaches worse in the evenings?** Two hypotheses, not one: (a) VOC emissions plus
   less ventilation in the evening; (b) the heater, rangehood and bathroom fan running together in a
   sealed house, so negative pressure draws flue gases into living spaces (IEA-155, IEA-158). The
   4:00 pm CO reading of 0 ppm was taken with the heater **off**, so it cannot test (b). CO has no
   smell (IEA-161), so the absence of a smell in the evening cannot test it either (IEA-162).
3. **Why can the assessor not settle hypothesis (b) on the visit?** Because negative-pressure and
   CO-spillage testing is gas service work for a gasfitter (IEA-159). The assessor does not create a
   possible CO exposure to test an idea. **Interim advice (IEA-156):** do not run exhaust fans at the
   same time as the heater. A CO alarm is a back-up, not a substitute (IEA-160).
4. **Why does the smell reach the bedrooms?** *Evidence:* bedroom TVOC 0.4, above outdoors. The
   relocated return-air grille beside the kitchen is a plausible pathway through the ducted system;
   draught-sealing means less outdoor air dilutes it.
5. **Why has ventilation fallen behind the emission load?** Draught-sealing reduced uncontrolled
   air leakage while new products were still off-gassing, and windows stay shut in winter. YourHome:
   ventilate rooms after new furnishings until the odour reduces considerably or disappears
   (IEA-153).

**Root causes to act on:** new-product emissions with reduced ventilation; and an unresolved
combustion-safety question. **Recommendations:** gasfitter (Victoria: a licensed gas plumber)
services the heater and checks for negative pressure before the next evening use (IEA-157); until
then, no exhaust fans with the heater (IEA-156); ventilate the kitchen daily (IEA-153); licensed
plumber checks the relocated floor waste; ducted-heating technician reviews the return-air
position; re-read the TVOC indicator at the same points in 2 weeks. If anyone has symptoms, see a
doctor; if the CO monitor alarms or anyone feels unwell with the heater on, everyone into fresh
air and call 000 (IEA-164).

**Stop rule:** if the personal CO monitor alarms at any point, stop, get everyone into fresh air,
turn the appliance off only if it is safe, and do not resume until a qualified person has checked
it (course stop-and-escalate card).

## 7. Student debrief

- **VOC is not CO.** The PID showed a VOC gradient; it cannot see CO. The CO monitor showed 0 ppm
  with the heater off; that cannot clear the heater in use. Both statements went in the report as
  written here.
- You raised two hypotheses for the evening headaches and did not choose between them without
  evidence; the combustion one went to a gasfitter, not to your own test.
- You compared indoor readings with outdoors and an unaffected room, never with a workplace list.
- The benzene question was dated: 1 ppm in May 2027, 0.2 ppm from 1 June 2027, workplace only.
- **New Zealand learners:** relocate the case to Wellington. A yellow flame, soot or a car-exhaust
  smell at a gas heater means turn it off and contact a licensed gas worker (IEA-163); suspected CO
  exposure means fresh air and immediate medical attention (IEA-164); NZ WES are guidance values
  for trained people (IEA-172).

## 8. Instructor notes

- **Common errors to mark down:** "CO confirmed" from an odour or a VOC reading; "no CO" from a
  heater-off reading or from no smell; running the heater with all fans on to "test" it; quoting a
  workplace limit (benzene or otherwise) as a home safe level; applying 0.2 ppm to a May 2027 job.
- **Practical stations:** (1) PID or TVOC indicator zeroed outdoors, then read at 0.5 m from a new
  panel and in a clean room; (2) trainer demonstration: smoke-pencil test of airflow at a doorway
  with an exhaust fan on and off, no gas appliance involved; (3) learner choice: which reading would
  separate the two evening hypotheses, and who takes it; (4) limitations exercise: list what a PID
  cannot tell you; (5) debrief with the key below. Instrument procedures: GAP IEA-209.
- **Quiz answer key** (`eebd60b3-…`): Q1 A, Q2 C, Q3 B, Q4 D, Q5 D, Q6 B, Q7 A, Q8 C, Q9 B
  (correctIndex 0, 2, 1, 3, 3, 1, 0, 2, 1). Unsafe options that must stay wrong: "Carbon monoxide
  confirmed by chemical odour", "Correct, no smell means no CO", "Yes, below the WEL means safe",
  "0.2 ppm (0.7 mg/m³) 8-hour TWA" (for May 2027) and "1 ppm (3.2 mg/m³) 8-hour TWA" (for June 2027).

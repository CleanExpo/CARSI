## 1. Case card (what the learner receives)

A 1970s weatherboard house in Hobart, Tasmania, in July. The tenants (two adults and a
five-month-old baby) report dark spotting where the main-bedroom ceiling meets the outside wall,
in the south-west corner. The spotting is about 0.4 m × 0.3 m (0.12 m²). The bedroom is
3.6 m × 3.2 m with a 2.4 m ceiling (27.6 m³). The family dries clothes on an airer in the bedroom
and keeps the window shut at night for security. A reverse-cycle split system heats the room.
The adjoining bathroom shower backs onto the bedroom's west wall. A tenant mentions the gutter
"overflowed in the last big storm". The property manager commissioned the visit: *What is causing
the bedroom corner to stay damp, and what should be done about it?*

Readings at 7:30 am (illustrative):

| Reading | Value | Instrument / mode | Location |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Air temperature, RH | 17.0 °C, 74 %RH | Thermo-hygrometer, stabilised 10 min | Bedroom, centre, 1.2 m |
| Air temperature, RH | 6.0 °C, 92 %RH | Same instrument | Outdoors, south side, shaded |
| Surface temperature | 10.5 °C | IR thermometer, emissivity set for painted plasterboard | Ceiling–wall corner |
| Surface temperature | 15.5 °C | Same | West wall, mid-height |
| Moisture (relative scale) | 38 corner / 12 reference | Non-invasive moisture meter, plasterboard scale | Corner vs internal hallway wall |
| Weather | No rain for 5 days | Bureau of Meteorology daily observations (learner checks) | Hobart |

Derived (worked in metric, IEA-114): bedroom dew point **12.3 °C**, **8.9 g/kg**; outdoors dew
point **4.8 °C**, **5.3 g/kg**. Indoor air carries **3.6 g/kg** more water than outdoor air.

## 3. Reasoned Five Whys

Each "why" is a question with the evidence needed to move on. The aim is a cause someone can fix,
not blame.

1. **Why is there spotting in the corner?** Because the surface stays damp long enough for growth.
   *Evidence:* damp to touch at 7:30 am; moisture 38 vs reference 12. enHealth lists leaking roofs
   and walls, faulty plumbing and condensation as common causes (IEA-105). Visible mould does not
   need testing (IEA-109).
2. **Why is the surface damp?** Either liquid water arrives (roof, gutter, plumbing, condensate) or
   vapour condenses. *Evidence:* the corner (10.5 °C) is below the room dew point (12.3 °C), so
   condensation is physically possible now. Five dry days with dampness that comes each morning and
   goes each afternoon weakens a roof or gutter leak; the shower test weakens a shower leak for that
   test (IEA-115). The split system is in heating mode and forms no indoor condensate (IEA-119).
3. **Why is this corner colder than the rest of the wall?** *Evidence:* insulation pulled back at
   the eave directly above it, and an external corner loses heat through two faces. A thermal bridge
   can lead to condensation where moist air meets the colder surface (IEA-113).
4. **Why is the room's dew point so high overnight?** *Evidence:* two adults and a baby in a closed
   27.6 m³ room, wet clothes drying on an airer, no exhaust, window shut: indoor 8.9 g/kg vs
   outdoor 5.3 g/kg.
5. **Why is the moisture not being removed?** *Evidence:* the window is kept shut for security and
   nothing else ventilates the room. enHealth: reduce and remove condensation, and opening a door or
   window is the cheapest way to reduce indoor moisture (IEA-116, IEA-117).

**Root causes to act on:** (a) missing ceiling insulation at the eave; (b) overnight moisture load
with no ventilation. **Recommendations tied to findings:** builder or insulation installer reinstates
the insulation (IEA-118); occupants dry clothes outside or in a vented space and ventilate the room
(a lockable window restrictor lets a window stay slightly open); clean the small affected area as
enHealth describes and control the source so it does not recur (IEA-110); re-read the corner surface
and the room after 2 weeks at the same time of day. If the gutter overflow recurs in rain, a roof
plumber checks it.

## 4. Plausible competing causes

| Cause | Supports it | Weakens or rules it out | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Condensation on a cold corner | Surface below dew point; damp mornings only; insulation gap | — | Supported |
| Roof or gutter leak | Tenant's storm report | Five dry days, damp every morning; dampness confined to the cold zone | Weakened, not closed |
| Shower or pipe leak | Shower behind the west wall | No change after 10-minute test | Weakened for this test |
| Split-system condensate | Unit in the room | Heating mode forms no indoor condensate in winter (IEA-119) | Unlikely this season |
| Rising or wind-driven damp in the wall | Weatherboard house | Damp zone is at the ceiling, not the floor; dry weather | Unlikely |

## 5. Referral boundaries

| Question or finding | Who | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insulation gap; building fabric | Builder or insulation installer | Trade work, not assessment (IEA-118) |
| Recurring gutter overflow | Roof plumber | Trade work, not assessment |
| Leak still suspected after more tests | Licensed plumber | Trade work, not assessment |
| Mould larger than a small area, or regrowth | Mould remediation professional | enHealth (IEA-111) |
| Hidden mould suspected, dispute, or owner wants an independent opinion | Occupational hygienist or other IEP | Outside the assessment's scope |
| Baby's cough | The family's doctor | Record as reported; no diagnosis (IEA-112) |
| Pets | Not applicable in this case | — |

**Stop rules met in this case:** none, provided the assessor does not enter the roof space. A
1970s house may contain asbestos (Australia: pre-1990 homes, IEA-088; NZ: pre-2000, IEA-089), so
the assessor looks from the manhole only.

## 6. Student debrief

- You separated what you saw from what you measured and from what you thought.
- The decisive measurement was a comparison: surface temperature against room dew point. No single
  humidity number is "safe"; enHealth says there is no exposure limit or health guideline value for
  mould (IEA-108).
- Each competing cause was tested and recorded as supported, weakened or unlikely, not "ruled out
  for good".
- The fix lies in the building and its use (insulation, ventilation, drying habits), so the
  recommendations go to a trade and to the occupants. Health questions go to a doctor.
- **New Zealand learners:** relocate the case to a Dunedin rental. The healthy homes ventilation
  standard requires openable windows, doors and skylights in each room to total at least 5 % of the
  floor area, and kitchens and bathrooms to have an extractor fan venting outside or qualifying
  continuous mechanical ventilation (IEA-120, IEA-121). Refer the landlord to Tenancy Services.

## 7. Instructor notes

- **Common errors to mark down:** writing "black mould" or "toxic mould" in the observation column;
  quoting a humidity or spore number as a safe limit; using a workplace exposure limit for a
  bedroom (IEA-171); closing the roof-leak hypothesis on one dry week; entering the roof space.
- **Marking guide (case write-up, if set as an assignment):** O/M/H table correct and complete
  (25 %); dew point and g/kg worked and compared with surfaces (25 %); Five Whys reaches a fixable
  cause with evidence at each step (25 %); referrals and limits stated (25 %). 80 % to pass.
- **Practical stations (founder addendum, 14/11/2026):**
  1. *Instrument station:* thermo-hygrometer stabilisation (record start and stable readings),
     IR thermometer emissivity setting on painted plasterboard vs a foil-faced surface, and a
     non-invasive moisture meter against an unaffected reference.
  2. *Trainer demonstration:* cold-surface condensation with a chilled metal tray in a humid box;
     the learner predicts the onset from the dew point.
  3. *Learner choice:* the learner picks the next measurement and says which hypothesis it tests.
  4. *Fault and limitations exercise:* a shiny surface read with the wrong emissivity setting gives a
     false surface temperature; a moisture meter on the wrong material scale gives a meaningless number.
  5. *Debrief:* answer key below.
  Manufacturer-specific procedures for each instrument are GAP IEA-209 and must be independently
  reviewed before the stations run.
- **Quiz answer key** (`1467ead9-…`): Q1 D, Q2 A, Q3 C, Q4 B, Q5 B, Q6 A, Q7 D, Q8 C, Q9 A
  (correctIndex 3, 0, 2, 1, 1, 0, 3, 2, 0). Unsafe options that must stay wrong (test
  `src/lib/courses/indoor-environment-case-quizzes.test.ts`): "Spore counts above 500 mean unsafe",
  "Use a workplace limit as threshold", "Toxic mould confirmed by sight alone".

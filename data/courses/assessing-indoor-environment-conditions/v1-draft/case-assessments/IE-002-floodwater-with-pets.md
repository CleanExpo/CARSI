## 1. Case card

Day 2 after riverine floodwater entered a 1960s low-set brick house in Ipswich, Queensland. The
water line on the internal walls is at 320 mm above the floor. A family of four lives there with
two dogs and a cat; they are staying with relatives. A council notice says a nearby sewer
overflowed during the flood. The electricity distributor disconnected supply; the house has
rooftop solar with a battery. The owner wants to "turn the power back on and run fans". One dog
drank floodwater on day 1 and has been lethargic since. A section of the back deck has dropped and
the stairs move underfoot. Debris has broken part of the fibre-cement eave lining. The insurer's
claims team asked for an indoor environment assessment: *What hazards must be controlled before
anyone re-enters to clean and dry, and who needs to be called?*

Readings at 10:00 am (illustrative):

| Reading | Value | Instrument / mode | Location |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Air temperature, RH | 27 °C, 85 %RH | Thermo-hygrometer, stabilised | Living room, 1.2 m |
| Air temperature, RH | 29 °C, 78 %RH | Same | Outdoors, shaded veranda |
| Moisture (relative scale) | 41 at 100 mm, 39 at 300 mm, 14 at 600 mm, 12 at 1,200 mm | Non-invasive meter, plasterboard scale | Living-room internal wall |
| Affected floor area | 6.0 m × 4.5 m = 27 m² (living) | Tape | Living room |
| Visual | No visible mould; no musty smell; strong sewage smell in the kitchen | — | Whole house |

Derived (IEA-144): indoors **19.1 g/kg** (dew point 24.3 °C); outdoors **19.7 g/kg** (dew point
24.8 °C). Opening the house removes little moisture today.

## 3. Reasoned Five Whys

1. **Why can nobody start cleaning today?** Because uncontrolled hazards remain: electricity,
   contaminated water and silt, structure and suspect asbestos. *Evidence:* the observations above.
2. **Why treat the water as contaminated without testing it?** Floodwater is often contaminated by
   sewage and agricultural or industrial wastes and chemicals (IEA-127); contact carries a higher
   infection risk (IEA-128); and the council has reported a sewer overflow. Sampling would answer no
   decision question, so none is taken (IEA-145).
3. **Why is the electrical system off-limits?** After flood damage a licensed electrician tests
   the installation before the distributor reconnects (IEA-134); a water-damaged switchboard is not
   touched (IEA-135); solar panels keep producing voltage in daylight and the battery may hold
   charge (IEA-136). Running fans now is not an option.
4. **Why is the dog's illness not part of the assessment?** Leptospirosis affects people and
   animals and spreads more easily after floods (IEA-130, IEA-131), but deciding what is wrong with
   the dog is a vet's job. The assessor records a non-clinical concern and urges a vet visit
   (IEA-132, IEA-133). People who handled the dog wash their hands (IEA-129).
5. **Why must drying start soon after it is safe?** Wet furniture and surfaces get mouldy after a
   couple of days (IEA-139). Mould is *not* present on day 2; the risk grows while materials stay
   wet, so the plan sequences safety first, then contaminated-material removal, then drying.

**Root causes to act on:** the flood itself is not controllable; what the plan controls is the
order of work. **Recommendations:** licensed electrician before any power; licensed asbestos
assessor or competent person for the eave lining before work near it; builder or structural
engineer for the deck and stairs; vet for the dog today; people stay out of the flooded areas and
keep children and animals away until cleaned (IEA-140); then a restoration contractor removes
contaminated materials and dries the building. No water blasters on silt (IEA-141).

**Stop rules met:** electrical, structural and suspect asbestos. No one enters the flooded rooms to
clean until the first two are cleared, and no one works near the eave lining until it is assessed.

## 6. Student debrief

- **Flood is not mould.** On day 2 you recorded "mould not observed; developing risk noted". Writing
  "mould present" because there was a flood is a false finding, and sampling to "confirm" it would
  have no decision value.
- You did not sample the floodwater: it is presumed contaminated on primary-source advice.
- You stopped at three hazards and named who clears each.
- You recorded the dog's condition as reported and sent it to a vet; you made no diagnosis.
- **New Zealand learners:** relocate the case to Gisborne. Health NZ: always assume floodwater is
  contaminated with farm run-off, animal and human sewage and chemicals (IEA-138); cleaning out
  buildings where animals have been may expose people to leptospirosis and salmonella (IEA-149);
  turn the power off at the mains and get an electrician to check before turning it on (IEA-137);
  health concerns go to a doctor or Healthline 0800 611 116 (IEA-148); emergencies 111.

## 7. Instructor notes

- **Common errors to mark down:** "mould present" on day 2; taking water or spore samples; advising
  the owner to restore power; naming the dog's disease; sweeping or water-blasting the eave debris;
  any workplace limit used as a household or pet level (IEA-171).
- **Practical stations:** (1) moisture mapping at 100, 300, 600 and 1,200 mm against an unaffected
  reference; (2) trainer demonstration of a hazard walk-round from outside the building only;
  (3) learner choice: order the referrals and justify the order; (4) limitations exercise: why a
  non-invasive meter reading on wet plasterboard over brick cannot show how wet the brick is;
  (5) debrief with the key below. Instrument procedures: GAP IEA-209.
- **Privacy:** record only that the owner reported an unwell dog and was advised to see a vet. No
  veterinary or medical records go into the report.
- **Quiz answer key** (`2c6669f8-…`): Q1 B, Q2 D, Q3 A, Q4 C, Q5 A, Q6 C, Q7 D, Q8 B, Q9 C
  (correctIndex 1, 3, 0, 2, 0, 2, 3, 1, 2). Unsafe options that must stay wrong: "Present, because
  every flood means mould", "Present; take spore samples to confirm", "Yes, if the safety switch
  holds", "Diagnose leptospirosis from the dog's symptoms".

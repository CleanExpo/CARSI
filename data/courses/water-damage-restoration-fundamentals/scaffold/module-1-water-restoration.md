### The idea
*(extracted from existing lesson content — to be expanded toward 300–600 words)*

Welcome to Water Damage Restoration Fundamentals. This course is for technicians, supervisors,
cleaners and insurance-support staff who attend water-damaged buildings and contents in Australia.
By the end you will be able to: describe what professional water damage restoration is and why a
structured process matters; categorise water by likely contamination (Category 1, 2 and 3) and
describe how the extent of intrusion is classified; identify site hazards and select PPE by the
hazard present, not by habit; take and interpret moisture readings with metric instruments and set
a defensible dry goal; explain the principles of structural drying and size equipment with realistic
power awareness at 230 V / 10 A; and produce documentation that stands up in an Australian insurance
and dispute context.

This is training leading to the CARSI Water Restoration Practitioner designation. It supports good
judgement and does not replace your own certification, a manufacturer's instructions, a Safety Data
Sheet (SDS), a relevant Australian standard, or a site's own rules.

## Lesson 1.2 — How the restoration mindset works

### On the job
[AUTHOR: 60–100 words. Australian scenario showing drying-vs-damage as a race. Not yet written.]

### The idea
*(extracted from existing lesson content — to be expanded toward 300–600 words)*

Water damage restoration is a race between drying and damage. Wet materials do not just look bad —
over hours and days they support microbial growth, corrosion, swelling and delamination. The
professional aim is to return the structure and restorable contents to a dry, safe condition
quickly, safely and with evidence, and to know when a material should be restored versus removed.

A reliable job follows a repeatable arc: make it safe, understand the water and the affected
materials, control the source and remove bulk water, assess moisture and set a dry goal, apply the
right drying strategy, monitor to the goal, then verify and document. Australia adds its own
constraints at every step — 230 V power and 10 A circuits, metric measurement, Australian climate
and building types, and Australian work-health-and-safety law. Where standards are named, we
reference them nominatively (for example, aligning to ANSI/IICRC S500) and never reproduce their
text.

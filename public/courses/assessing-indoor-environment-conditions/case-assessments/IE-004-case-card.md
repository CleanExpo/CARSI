# Case card IE-004 — Light commercial renovation dust

**CARSI Indoor Environment Assessment Practitioner — case assessment (Module 5)**
*DRAFT for SME review. A fictional CARSI teaching case with illustrative readings. Completing it is
a learning activity only: it does not show competency or give anyone a health clearance.*

## The job

Toowoomba, Queensland, March 2027. A single-storey 1978 shopping strip. A medical-centre tenancy
is being refitted while the pharmacy next door keeps trading. The tenancies share one ceiling void
and one ducted air-conditioning return. Pharmacy staff report fine grey dust on shelves and sore
eyes. The property manager asks: *Where is the dust coming from, and what must happen to stop it?*

From the public side of the hoarding you see:

- shopfitters cutting fibre-cement wall lining (3.0 m × 4.8 m) with a circular saw; no asbestos
  register on site;
- an electrician chasing a brick wall with an angle grinder, no dust extraction;
- a painter dry-sanding original painted timber window frames;
- a carpenter cutting softwood framing; a labourer mixing Portland cement;
- a diesel forklift in the rear loading dock, doors open to the tenancy;
- the hoarding stops at the ceiling grid, leaving the void above it open (6.0 m × 0.3 m).

## Readings (illustrative)

## Your tasks

1. Fill in **observation**, **measurement** and **hypothesis** columns.
2. Name the stop triggers in the order you act on them, and who you refer each to.
3. Work out the fibre-cement area and say what it means for who may remove it, if it is asbestos.
4. Write a Five Whys chain from the dust on the shelves to a cause someone can fix.
5. *Occupational extension:* for Queensland in March and May 2027, state the arrangement that
   applies to softwood dust, Portland cement and diesel particulate matter, with dates and units.
   Explain why none of these figures tells the pharmacy it is "clear".
6. *New Zealand learners:* relocate the case to Hamilton and apply the NZ positions.

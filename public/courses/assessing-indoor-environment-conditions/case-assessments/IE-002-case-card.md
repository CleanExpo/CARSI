# Case card IE-002 — Floodwater in a family home with pets

**CARSI Indoor Environment Assessment Practitioner — case assessment (Module 4)**
*DRAFT for SME review. A fictional CARSI teaching case with illustrative readings. Completing it is
a learning activity only: it does not show competency or give anyone a health clearance.*

## The job

Day 2 after riverine floodwater entered a 1960s low-set brick house in Ipswich, Queensland. The
water line on internal walls is 320 mm above the floor. A family of four and their two dogs and a
cat are staying with relatives. A council notice says a nearby sewer overflowed. The electricity
distributor has disconnected supply; the house has rooftop solar and a battery. The owner wants to
"turn the power back on and run fans". One dog drank floodwater on day 1 and has been lethargic
since. Part of the back deck has dropped and the stairs move. Debris has broken part of the
fibre-cement eave lining. The insurer asks: *What hazards must be controlled before anyone
re-enters to clean and dry, and who needs to be called?*

## Your tasks

1. Work out g/kg indoors and outdoors and say what it means for drying today.
2. Fill in **observation**, **measurement** and **hypothesis** columns. Record only what you saw
   about mould.
3. Decide whether any sampling would answer a real question here, and say why.
4. Write a Five Whys chain that ends with the order of work.
5. List every stop trigger and who clears it (electrical, structural, suspect asbestos, people's
   health, the dog's health).
6. *New Zealand learners:* relocate the case to a flooded Gisborne house and apply the NZ
   positions.

Then take the IE-002 quiz (pass mark 80 %).

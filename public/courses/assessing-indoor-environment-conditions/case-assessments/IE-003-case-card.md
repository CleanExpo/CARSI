# Case card IE-003 — Odour after a kitchen renovation and HVAC changes

**CARSI Indoor Environment Assessment Practitioner — case assessment (Module 3)**
*DRAFT for SME review. A fictional CARSI teaching case with illustrative readings. Completing it is
a learning activity only: it does not show competency or give anyone a health clearance.*

## The job

A two-storey townhouse in Melbourne, Victoria, in June. Three weeks ago the owners finished a
kitchen renovation: new particleboard and MDF cabinets with painted doors, a high-capacity ducted
rangehood, a second bathroom exhaust fan and draught-sealing of doors and windows. The ducted
heating return-air grille was moved beside the kitchen, and the sink was moved with a re-run floor
waste. An open-flued gas space heater in the living room runs most evenings. Two adults and a
14-year-old with asthma report a "chemical" smell and headaches "worse in the evenings". The
owners ask: *What is causing the smell and the evening headaches, and what should happen next?*

## Your tasks

1. Fill in **observation**, **measurement** and **hypothesis** columns. Say exactly what the VOC
   reading and the CO reading can and cannot tell you.
2. Give at least two hypotheses for the evening headaches and say who tests each.
3. Write a Five Whys chain with the evidence for each step.
4. List the interim advice you pass on and the referrals (gas, plumbing, ducted heating, health).
5. *Occupational extension:* the renovation company asks which benzene limit applies to its
   workers on a separate commercial job in May 2027, and in June 2027. Answer with jurisdiction,
   date, units and averaging time, and say why the figure never applies to the home.
6. *New Zealand learners:* relocate the case to Wellington and apply the NZ gas-safety position.

Then take the IE-003 quiz (pass mark 80 %).

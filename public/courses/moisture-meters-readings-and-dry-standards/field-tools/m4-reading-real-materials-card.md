# Reading real materials: field card (one page)

**CARSI Professional Moisture Meters — field tool (Module 4)**
*Australian-produced: metric readings throughout.*

## Field checklist

- [ ] Framing: several studs, three heights (near the base, about 300 mm, about 1 m), plus the bottom plate and a control stud in an unaffected wall
- [ ] Framing: measure the wood temperature and correct each reading the way your meter maker specifies, once only (skip a manual correction if the meter already corrects)
- [ ] Plasterboard: read a known-dry patch of the same board first (the baseline), then trace and mark the boundary
- [ ] Plasterboard: re-read the baseline every visit
- [ ] Concrete: check the slab temperature against the room dew point; record surface readings as qualitative
- [ ] Concrete: before any hole is drilled, get the point cleared for in-slab heating, services, reinforcement and post-tensioned cables; if it cannot be cleared, do not drill there
- [ ] Concrete: specify a hygrometer test to the named method; the floor covering maker and the standard named in the job specification set the limit
- [ ] Odd number? Run the false-reading table below before you record it

## Formulas and units

| Quantity | Formula | Units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relining check (QBCC) | all framing readings, corrected, below 16% and the framing sound | % MC |
| Temperature correction (resistance meter) | about 0.5% per 5.6 °C; add when the wood is colder than the calibration temperature, subtract when warmer; use your maker's method if it gives one, and skip it if the meter already corrects | % MC |
| Plasterboard boundary | where readings return to the same-day unaffected baseline | points |
| Slab surface check | slab temperature against room dew point | °C |
| Probe depth, slab drying from one side (ARFA TB001) | 40% of the slab thickness (example: 80 mm in a 200 mm slab); take the real figure from the specified method | mm |

## False-reading table

| Symptom | Possible cause | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High in a straight line on a wall (pinless) | Nail line, metal stud, angle bead or pipe | Check the line itself by a suitable method once you know what is behind it; beside the line is only the control. Unchecked stays unverified |
| High on treated timber or after salt water | Salts, treatment or seawater | Compare with untreated timber nearby; track the trend |
| Low in a cold space, high in a hot one | No temperature correction | Measure wood temperature and correct (unless the meter already corrects) |
| Jumping readings | Poor contact, low battery or wrong mode | Clean electrodes, check battery and mode, re-read |
| Dry-looking surface, wet material underneath (false low) | Dry skin over a wet core | Read at depth with insulated pins |

## EPS in concrete: screed, pod or void?

1. White beads through the whole hole wall and all the spoil: an EPS bead screed or fill. Identify the product and layer, and use the test method and limits its maker accepts for the floor finish. Do not apply a normal-concrete scale or correction.
2. Dense concrete in the hole wall and solid foam at the bottom: the hole broke into a pod or void. **Record that point as invalid for concrete RH.** Do not move the probe up or drill deeper to rescue it.
3. A pod is not part of the concrete thickness. Set the depth from the concrete section at that exact point (rib or pod), not the nominal slab thickness.
4. Before drilling: confirm the build-up, and get the point cleared for post-tensioned cables, reinforcement and services. Control dust at the drill.
5. No safe, representative point? Record **"no valid in-situ result"** and seek an alternative accepted for that floor assembly. A calcium chloride test is an alternative only where it suits that floor and the specification and floor covering maker accept it. Never substitute a surface reading and call the floor ready.

## Glossary

- **Wet boundary:** the line where readings return to the unaffected baseline.
- **Flooring hygrometer:** an instrument that measures RH in or under a slab for a dryness test.
- **False high:** a reading raised by something other than moisture, such as metal, salts or heat.

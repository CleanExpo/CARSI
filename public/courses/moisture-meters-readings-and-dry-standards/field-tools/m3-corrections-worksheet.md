# Temperature and species corrections worksheet

**CARSI Professional Moisture Meters — field tool (Module 3)**
*Australian-produced: metric readings throughout.*

## The rule (resistance meters, timber)

Correct by about **0.5% moisture content for each 5.6 °C** the wood temperature differs from the meter's calibration temperature. **Add** for wood colder than calibration; **subtract** for wood warmer than calibration. Newer dielectric meters correct for temperature internally.

`MC corrected = MC read + 0.5 × (T cal − T wood) ÷ 5.6`

Use the calibration temperature in your meter's manual. The worked examples in the course assume **20 °C**.

## Steps

1. Identify the species (ask the builder or floor supplier if unsure).
2. Measure the **wood temperature** at the point you read.
3. Take the reading and note the read height (mm) and pin depth (mm).
4. Apply the species correction from **your meter's** table or setting, in the order your maker specifies. This course does not reproduce any maker's numbers.
5. Apply the temperature correction.
6. Check the notes column: treated timber? salt water or seawater? surface water? pins oriented as the maker instructs?
7. Remember: above 30% a reading stays qualitative whatever the correction.

## Why readings run high: check before you conclude

- Dissolved salts or seawater
- Preservative or fire-retardant treatment
- Warm wood with no temperature correction
- Surface water on uninsulated pins
- Pins not oriented as the maker instructs

Then compare with the same material unaffected in the same building, and track the trend rather than the single number.

## Glossary

- **Calibration temperature:** the wood temperature at which the meter reads without correction (see your manual).
- **Species correction:** the maker's adjustment for a species' density and electrical behaviour.
- **Electrolytes:** dissolved salts, treatments or seawater that raise conductivity and readings.

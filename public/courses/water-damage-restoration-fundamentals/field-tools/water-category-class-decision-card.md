# Water category and class decision card

**CARSI Water Restoration Practitioner — field tool (Module 2)**
*DRAFT v1 for SME review. Australian-produced: metric units, Australian sources and conditions.*

## Category — how contaminated (drives PPE, cleaning, disposal)

| Category | Plain meaning | Typical Australian sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Category 1** | Clean water | Burst mains supply line, overflowing tap, clean rainwater ingress |
| **Category 2** | Grey water (could cause illness) | Dishwasher or washing-machine discharge, toilet overflow with urine, no faeces |
| **Category 3** | Black water (harmful agents) | Sewage, water beyond the toilet trap, river or sea floodwater |

**Two rules:** a category can **worsen over time** in a warm, closed building — reassess. When **unsure, treat as the worse category**. Record the source, contamination seen, time elapsed and conditions, and the reasons for your call.

## Class — how much water / how hard to dry (drives equipment and time)

- **Class 1** — least water; part of a room or low-porosity materials. Fastest.
- **Class 2** — a whole room; carpet and underlay; wall wicking to about **300 mm**.
- **Class 3** — greatest amount; ceilings, walls, insulation, subfloor saturated, often from above.
- **Class 4** — specialty: bound water in hardwood, plaster, masonry, concrete. Slow; specialist methods.

**Class ready-reckoner:** wetted footprint (m²) · wall wicking height (mm) · did water come from above? · low-permeability materials involved?

Record **category and class together** and revisit both as materials are opened up.

# Australia and New Zealand hazardous-materials card

**CARSI Fire & Smoke Restoration Practitioner — field tool (Module 3)**
*DRAFT v1 for SME review. Australian and New Zealand. 230 V / 50 Hz, metric, AS/NZS. Standards named only (aligned to the principles of ANSI/IICRC S700:2025); no standard text reproduced.*

## Asbestos

| | Australia | New Zealand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suspect asbestos if built or renovated | before **31 December 2003** (national ban); most likely before 1990 | before **2000** |
| Asbestos register | workplaces in buildings built before 31 December 2003 (Qld: before 31 December 1989; Vic: own rules) | asbestos management plan for workplace buildings built before 1 January 2000 |
| Fire-damaged material | Treated as friable: NSW EPA says fire-impacted ACM is friable and Class A work; WorkSafe Victoria says assume friable or have a competent person inspect | Reclassify as friable; Class A removalist (WorkSafe NZ) |
| Class A licence | all types, including friable and asbestos-contaminated dust and debris | any type or quantity, including friable and ACD |
| Class B licence | non-friable | non-friable (over 10 m² needs Class B or A) |
| No licence | 10 m² or less of non-friable (ACT: licence for any removal) | up to 10 m² of non-friable, counted over the whole project; no splitting into sectors |
| Emergency removal after a fire | check your state or territory regulator | licensed removalist notifies WorkSafe immediately by phone, in writing within 24 hours |

**Restorer's rule, both countries:** a later build date never proves a building is asbestos-free, and you cannot tell asbestos by looking. On suspect fire-damaged material: **stop, do not disturb, keep people out, hand over** to a licensed assessor and Class A removalist. Clean that area only after clearance.

## Lead paint

| Australia | New Zealand |
| --- | --- |
| Lead paint used in many homes before **1970**; newer homes may also have it | Presume lead-based paint in buildings from the **1980s or earlier** (WorkSafe NZ, Health NZ) |

Test or presume. No open-flame torches (lead fumes). No dry sanding or dry sweeping. HEPA vacuum and wet wipe. Keep pregnant women and young children away. Lead dust may sit in ceiling cavities that fire or demolition opens.

## Respirable crystalline silica (concrete, brick, stone, mortar, fibre-cement)

| Australia | New Zealand |
| --- | --- |
| **0.05 mg/m³** 8-hour TWA; mandatory; unchanged at the 1 December 2026 move to Workplace Exposure Limits | **0.025 mg/m³** (from November 2023); WES are advisory, but the HSWA duty to eliminate or minimise still applies |

Wet cutting and on-tool extraction first. Respirators to AS/NZS 1716, selected under AS/NZS 1715, fit-tested before first use and fit-checked every time.

## Treated-timber (CCA) ash

- Treat it as containing copper, chromium and arsenic: up to **10 % by weight**.
- Gloves, disposable coveralls, P2 mask. Moisten it; shovel, do not sweep; no high-pressure water.
- Double-bag, seal and take to landfill as your council directs. Never bury it or put it in a garden or organics bin.
- **New Zealand:** NZ EPA guidance says not to burn CCA-treated wood because of toxic smoke and ash. Check your regional or district council for disposal.

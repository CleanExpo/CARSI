# Power and electrical safety worksheet (AU/NZ)

**CARSI Structural Drying Practitioner — field tool (Module 5)**
*DRAFT v1 for SME review. Australian and New Zealand job conditions: 230 V / 50 Hz, 10 A GPO, metric units.*

## 1. Drying-system decision

Inside ______ g/kg. Outside ______ g/kg at ______ °C.

- [ ] **Closed** (default): seal the zone and dehumidify.
- [ ] **Open**: only if outside g/kg is lower **and** the outside air is warm enough not to chill the materials.
- [ ] **Heat-assisted**: electric heat only, on its own budgeted circuit.

**Carbon monoxide rule:** no fuel-burning heaters, generators or petrol-powered equipment inside, in either country. New Zealand's workplace exposure standard for carbon monoxide is 20 ppm (8-hour average); in Australia, check the current Safe Work Australia exposure limit.

## 2. Power budget — 230 V, 10 A circuits

## 3. Electrical safety checklist

**Australia**
- [ ] Every lead and portable machine on RCD protection (socket RCD up to 20 A trips at no more than 30 mA)
- [ ] Equipment inspected and tested by a competent person; tags current (AS/NZS 3760:2022)

**New Zealand**
- [ ] Every lead and appliance RCD-protected — a wet job is a damp condition (Electricity (Safety) Regulations 2010)
- [ ] Equipment electrically safe and maintained; testing and tagging to AS/NZS 3760 is one way to show it (not mandatory)

**Both**
- [ ] Every cord and plug checked visually each visit
- [ ] Generator (if any) outside, away from openings — never indoors or in an internal garage

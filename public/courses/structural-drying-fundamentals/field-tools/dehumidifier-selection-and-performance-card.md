# Dehumidifier selection and performance card

**CARSI Structural Drying Practitioner — field tool (Module 4)**
*DRAFT v1 for SME review. Australian and New Zealand job conditions: 230 V / 50 Hz, 10 A GPO, metric units.*

## 1. Conditions check

Room ______ °C, ______ %RH → dew point ______ °C.

- Warm, humid (dew point well above 10 °C): refrigerant or LGR suits.
- Cold (dew point near 10 °C or below): a refrigerant coil will run near freezing and can ice. Consider a **desiccant**, **electric heat**, or both.

Desiccant chosen? - [ ] Reactivation (wet) air ducted **out of the drying zone**, usually outside.

Units built for 230 V, 50 Hz supply, sourced through an Australian or New Zealand distributor. Rating plate read: ______ A.

## 2. Litres per day from your readings

| Line | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Process airflow (m³/h) | |
| × air density 1.2 kg/m³ = air mass (kg/h) | |
| Inlet g/kg | |
| Outlet g/kg | |
| Depression (inlet − outlet) g/kg | |
| Water removed (kg/h) = air mass × depression ÷ 1000 | |
| × 24 = litres per day | |

Worked: 340 m³/h × 1.2 = 408 kg/h; × 3.7 g/kg ÷ 1000 = 1.51 kg/h; × 24 = about 36 L/day.

## 3. Cross-check

Condensate logged (pump counter or emptied bucket): ______ L since last visit.
Big gap from the calculation? Check filter, outlet, pump line and meter.

A daily figure that falls as the room dries is expected, provided the materials are approaching their dry standard.

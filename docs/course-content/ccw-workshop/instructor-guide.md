## 2. Wording rules for the room (licence-critical)

CARSI is accredited as a CEC provider. That standing covers continuing education credits only. It does not cover running the schools and exams that lead to a certification. Say and project only the wording below.

- Say "IICRC CEC Accredited" or "CARSI is an IICRC CEC provider". Never drop the word CEC from that phrase, and never describe CARSI or its courses as approved by the IICRC.
- Never say this workshop earns CECs, and never quote a number of CEC hours.
- Never say or imply that CARSI certifies anyone or runs the exams that lead to a certification. A certification comes only through IICRC-approved schools and examinations.
- Do not use IICRC discipline acronyms to name or describe this workshop or its participants. You may say, as a third-person fact, that an IICRC certification exists, and you may name a standard by its number.
- Do not read standard text aloud or paste it on a slide. Name the standard and refer participants to the IICRC.
- Never make an absence claim about a standard, such as "the standard never mentions X". You cannot prove it from the index.
- Four deck slides (2, 4, 38, 40) contain wording that breaks these rules (a line that describes CARSI as approved by the IICRC, a 90-plus course count, a "CARSI-delivered" certification step on the pathway ladder, and a CEC maintenance figure). Do not read those lines out. Fix the deck before the next delivery.
- Three deck slides (14, 24, 29) quote sentences from IICRC standards. Do not read them out. Replace them in the deck with CARSI wording.

## 4. Safety briefing script (deliver before any hands-on work, Day 1, then a short repeat on Day 2)

Read this aloud, in your own words if you prefer. Keep it under five minutes.

> Before we touch a machine or a bottle, here is what you need to know.
>
> Every one of us is a chemical handler today. Gloves and glasses stay on at every station, and so do closed shoes. Nitrile gloves, minimum. If a station asks for a P2 respirator, you wear it.
>
> Every chemical on the tables has a Safety Data Sheet in the folder at the front. Read the first aid section of the products you use. If anyone is splashed or breathes something they should not, tell me straight away. The Poisons Information line is 13 11 26.
>
> Every bottle you fill gets a label with the product, the hazard and the date. No unlabelled bottles, ever.
>
> Never mix chemicals. In particular, never mix an acid with chlorine, and never mix an oxidiser with a reducing agent.
>
> All the power runs through safety switches. Press the test button on the portable machine at the start of each station. Keep leads off wet floor and run them along the walls. If a lead is damaged, stop. Tell me.
>
> The floor will be wet. Wet floor signs mean walk slowly. Do not run.
>
> The truck-mount is loud. Hearing protection goes on before it starts. The engine never runs in an enclosed space, because of carbon monoxide.
>
> It is a physical two days. If you feel hot, dizzy or unwell, sit out and tell me. We rotate stations to share the load.
>
> The one thing that matters most: if you are not sure, stop. Ask.

New Zealand participants: where this course names an Australian regulator, the New Zealand equivalent is WorkSafe New Zealand under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015. Direct them to worksafe.govt.nz and epa.govt.nz and do not quote New Zealand thresholds from memory.

## 6. Module teaching notes

Each note gives the slides, the teaching points, the station set-up and the equipment. Items marked (suggested) are not in the source deck and are the trainer's call.

### Module 1: Welcome to the Workshop: Safety and Standards

Time: 60 minutes. Slides 1 to 8.

Teaching points:

- Open with slide 1 and the line "this is a workshop, not a lecture". Slide 2 introduces the trainer. Keep credibility factual and skip any claim about course counts.
- Slide 3: walk the agenda in about 90 seconds. Stress the hands-on work each afternoon.
- Slide 4: the three levels. Tell the room that nobody will be taught below their level or pushed above it without support. Say the level descriptions in your own words and leave out the certification acronym that the slide names.
- Slide 7: the standards scope table. Tie each standard to what a carpet technician does and when to hand off.
- Slide 8: safety. Spend ten minutes on the water category scope (Category 1, 2, 3). A technician working near Category 3 water must stop and call a restoration firm.
- Run the safety briefing from section 4 here.

Station set-up and equipment: Safety folder with a Safety Data Sheet for every product on the tables. First aid kit and eyewash on site. Spare PPE: nitrile gloves, safety glasses, P2 respirators, hearing protection. Wet floor signs. Printed attendance list and level self-assessment sheets.

### Module 2: Fibre Science

Time: 75 minutes. Slides 9 to 11.

Teaching points:

- The burn test is the highest-value demonstration of the course. Wool self-extinguishes and smells like hair, polyester beads black, nylon hardens to a grey bead and cellulose burns like paper.
- Slide 10 lists the fibres. Run the four stations: burn, float, solubility, hand-feel.
- Slide 11 is the fibre fence table. It becomes a laminated field card. Stress the rayon wet-strength loss. Note that the CARSI manual table is the reference for pH and temperature limits.
- Teach the weakest-member rule with one live blend example.

Station set-up and equipment: Labelled swatches of wool, silk, cotton or linen, rayon, nylon, polyester, olefin, with two unlabelled mystery tufts. Fire-safe tray, lighter, tweezers, a bowl of cool water, an extinguisher and a water spray bottle within reach. Acetone for the solubility station, used only with ventilation and no ignition sources. Printed fibre-ID field card.

### Module 3: Chemistry of Cleaning

Time: 90 minutes. Slides 12 to 15.

Teaching points:

- Slide 13: the pH scale and the golden rule. Target rinse pH is 6.5 to 8.0 on the substrate. Press a strip to a damp spot, not into the bottle.
- Slide 14: CHAT. Give the three worked examples from the lesson. Pros know that when one dial is constrained, such as heat on wool, the others must make up for it.
- Teach the WoolSafe point carefully: pH 7 does not mean wool-safe. The criterion is alkalinity.
- Slide 15: the take-away by level. Repeat this three-level close at the end of every module.

Station set-up and equipment: pH strips (wide range and narrow range), distilled water, white towels, labelled spray bottles with samples at published dilutions, the CCW product guide printed, and a Safety Data Sheet folder. Gloves, glasses, P2 respirator at the mixing table.

### Module 4: The Five Cleaning Principles

Time: 105 minutes. Slides 16 to 18.

Teaching points:

- This is the centre of gravity of the workshop: four stations, 25 minutes each, full rotation.
- Enforce the rule that the top half of the pre-inspection form is completed before anyone sprays. It builds the habit.
- Station A: weigh the dry soil removed with each machine, and run a white cloth along a pile row to set the baseline.
- Station B: low-moisture methods. Station C: hot water extraction, truck-mount against portable, with a water-lift and pressure reading each. Station D: drying, airmover placement and dehumidifier sizing.
- Lightly soiled residential work is mostly Principle 1. Heavily soiled commercial work leans on Principles 2 and 3.
- Slide 17 quotes a figure for the share of soil that is dry. It does not match the manual's single-pass figures. Teach the manual figures and let Station A produce the real numbers.

Station set-up and equipment: Station A: upright vacuum, pile lifter, a third machine, a set of kitchen scales, white cloths, pre-soiled carpet panels. Station B: encapsulation product and a rotary machine with bonnet, dry compound and a vacuum. Station C: a truck-mount, a portable, and vacuum and pressure gauges, wand with dry-stroke markings. Station D: airmovers, a dehumidifier, a thermo-hygrometer and a moisture meter. All stations: pre-inspection forms (top half), RCD-protected power, cable covers and wet floor signs.

### Module 5: Spot and Stain Removal

Time: 105 minutes. Slides 19 to 21.

Teaching points:

- Slide 19: a spot comes out and a stain is the fibre itself changed. They are different fights.
- Slide 20: the stain classes and the five-step matrix: identify, test, cold chemistry, heat or oxidiser, dry fast.
- Teach blot, do not rub, with a live demonstration of pile distortion.
- Teach the aged urine point: acid-side first.
- Slide 21: pros know when to walk away. Teach the three-attempt rule and the honest disclosure.

Station set-up and equipment: The six-bottle spotting kit at every station (acid-side spotter, alkaline-side spotter, enzyme spotter, POG solvent, oxidiser, reducing agent), white towels, blotters, labelled test swatches stained with each class (suggested), gloves and glasses, and a no-mix reminder card.

### Module 6: Upholstery Theory: Fibre and Fabric Construction

Time: 90 minutes. Slides 22 to 24.

Teaching points:

- Slide 22 opens Day 2. Slide 23: if you do nothing else, run bleed, crock, shrink, distortion.
- Slide 24: source the demonstration fabrics beforehand. A non-colourfast wool and rayon blend will crock visibly.
- Do not teach the cleaning code as a guarantee. Teach it as a starting point.
- The shrink test is read at 24 hours. Wet and dry a patch at the end of Day 1 (suggested) so participants can read a 24-hour result on Day 2.

Station set-up and equipment: Demonstration fabrics including one that will bleed and crock. White cloths, droppers, a 50 by 50 mm template, a ruler, low-airflow fans, pre-wetted patches from Day 1, and the four-test record sheet.

### Module 7: Upholstery Hands-On

Time: 135 minutes. Slides 25 and 26.

Teaching points:

- Slide 25 lists the six methods. Run all six live on matched test pieces so the contrast is visible.
- Speed-drying and dwell discipline are the two outcomes to feel.
- No live steam. Check the temperature at the tool.
- Olefin: dial the heat down and keep the wand moving. Have participants check the real jet temperature on the machine.

Station set-up and equipment: Six matched test pieces, one per method. Hand tool, stainless hand wand and an air-driven tool. Airmovers or fans, a thermo-hygrometer, and a small dehumidifier. Dry solvent, dry compound, dry foam, encapsulation, shampoo, hot water extraction supplies. Ventilation for the solvent station and no ignition sources.

### Module 8: Hard Floors

Time: 75 minutes. Slides 27 to 29.

Teaching points:

- Slide 27 and 28: the five families. Calcareous against siliceous is the main lesson. Ten seconds of vinegar on marble etches it for good.
- The inspection is non-destructive. Photograph and caption every observation.
- Slide 29: the inspection tool list. Skip the quoted sentence on the slide.
- Be honest about the product guide: stone-safe and hard-floor chemistry is not a major line at the partner counter, so participants should ask or use a specialist supplier.

Station set-up and equipment: Samples of marble (or limestone) and granite, a sealed timber piece, a laminate piece, tile with grout and vinyl. A labelled squeeze bottle of distilled white vinegar with a dropper, pH strips, a moisture meter, a thermo-hygrometer, a camera and a scale reference card.

### Module 9: Machinery and Preventive Maintenance

Time: 90 minutes. Slides 30 and 31.

Teaching points:

- The best chemistry cannot outrun a worn blower. Plan 45 minutes truck-mount, 30 minutes portable and 15 minutes wand and hose (see the timing note in section 5).
- Teach the daily list, then the weekly and monthly ones, from slide 31, then the machine-specific schedules in the LMS lesson.
- Teach the 300-hour trap: read the engine plate and follow that engine's manual.
- Show the brush measurement: new about 25 mm, condemn at about 8 mm.
- Hand out the maintenance log template.

Station set-up and equipment: A running truck-mount, a portable extractor, a wand and a hose set, a worn and a new vacuum brush pair, a ruler, a copy of one operator manual, vacuum gauges and pressure gauges, and blank machine logs.

### Module 10: The Business of Cleaning

Time: 60 minutes. Slides 32 to 34.

Teaching points:

- Slide 33: the six cost lines. Slide 34: role-play the three complaint scripts in pairs, five minutes each, then swap.
- The deck mentions a pricing spreadsheet in the take-home pack. Confirm it exists before you promise it (section 11).
- Callback benchmarks of 3 to 4 per cent, above 6 per cent and below 1 per cent are CARSI professional judgement, not survey data. Say so.
- Deck figures on typical margin and travel time are unsourced. Do not quote them.

Station set-up and equipment: Printed complaint scripts, a sample moisture addendum, a calculator or the costing sheet if confirmed, and a timer.

### Module 11: Expert Board Case Review

Time: 45 minutes. Slides 35 to 37.

Teaching points:

- Slide 36: the three stages. Credit Andrej Karpathy for the underlying pattern.
- Slide 37: the seven roles. Stress that these are composite archetypes and not real people.
- Use the teaching case in the LMS lesson, or bring a real anonymised case.
- Finish with the take-home pack (slide 38) and the close (slides 39 to 41). Leave out the certification pathway ladder on slide 40 and the CEC maintenance figure (see section 2).

Station set-up and equipment: Printed persona cards, index cards, a timer and the take-home packs.

### Modules 12 to 16 (not delivered in the room)

These are self-paced. Point participants to them at the close. If a participant asks about strip and seal on an old floor, repeat the asbestos stop rule: pre-1990 resilient tile of unknown history is stop, do not disturb, and refer.

## 7. Assessment and sign-off

- Each of Modules 1 to 15 has a quiz in the LMS. The pass mark is 80 per cent and a participant has three attempts.
- The certificate of attendance depends on check-in on both days. It does not depend on the quiz result. Tell participants this. Confirm with CARSI whether quiz results should be recorded for any other purpose (section 11).
- During the hands-on modules (4, 7, 8, 9) watch each participant and sign off the points below on your trainer sheet. A participant who has not shown a point gets one more supervised attempt before the end of the module.

Hands-on sign-off points:

- Module 2: identifies a mystery fibre using hand-feel, a burn test and a float test, and names the correct fibre.
- Module 4: completes the top half of the pre-inspection form before spraying. Vacuums in two directions. Uses the dry-stroke technique. Sets an airmover plan.
- Module 5: names the stain class, tests first, then blots and follows the decision tree in order. Names the three-attempt rule.
- Module 6: runs and records all four tests, and reads the shrink test at 24 hours.
- Module 7: picks a method for a given piece and explains why. No live steam. Checks the temperature at the tool on olefin.
- Module 8: runs the vinegar drop test and records the result with a dated photograph.
- Module 9: completes the daily checklist and measures a brush.
Record the sign-offs on the trainer sheet and keep it with the attendance list. A sign-off point is an observation. It is not a certification of competence.

## 10. Answer key

Correct options are lettered in the order they appear in the LMS. Every answer is supported by the lesson text.

### Module 1: Welcome to the Workshop: Safety and Standards

1. Which standard is the primary reference for cleaning carpet and other textile floor coverings?
   Answer: B. ANSI/IICRC S100
2. A kitchen flood has left Category 3 (grossly contaminated) water in a carpet. What should you do?
   Answer: A. Stop, photograph it, document it and refer it to a restorer
3. How should every decanted chemical container be labelled?
   Answer: D. The product, the hazard and the date
4. What do you do with the test button on the RCD protecting a portable extractor?
   Answer: C. Press it at the start of every job
5. Where must a Safety Data Sheet be kept for each hazardous chemical you use?
   Answer: B. On site, in the vehicle
6. Why do you wear hearing protection around a truck-mount?
   Answer: A. Truck-mounts are loud, typically 85 dBA or more
7. Soot on a carpet after a fire is best handled by:
   Answer: D. Referring the job to a restorer

### Module 2: Fibre Science

1. Which fibre family does wool belong to?
   Answer: C. Protein
2. A tuft burns slowly, self-extinguishes, leaves crushable black ash and smells like burnt hair. It is most likely:
   Answer: B. Wool
3. A tuft melts to a hard grey bead and smells like celery. It is most likely:
   Answer: A. Nylon
4. Which fibre floats in cool water?
   Answer: D. Olefin (polypropylene)
5. What is the maximum pH for nylon in this course?
   Answer: C. 10
6. What is the maximum water temperature for wool in the CARSI fibre table?
   Answer: B. 40 °C
7. You are cleaning an 80 per cent wool, 20 per cent nylon carpet. Which fibre decides the chemistry?
   Answer: A. Wool, because the more delicate fibre governs
8. Roughly how much strength can rayon lose when wet, according to the manual?
   Answer: D. Up to 70 per cent

### Module 3: Chemistry of Cleaning

1. What does CHAT stand for?
   Answer: C. Chemistry, Heat, Agitation, Time
2. What is the pH ceiling for nylon?
   Answer: B. 10
3. Which surfactant family damages the acid-dye blockers on nylon?
   Answer: A. Cationic
4. What final substrate pH should you aim for after extraction?
   Answer: D. 6.5 to 8.0
5. A pH strip dipped in your pre-spray bottle tells you:
   Answer: C. The chemistry of the bottle, not what the fibre holds
6. What is the WoolSafe criterion for an alkaline pre-spray based on?
   Answer: B. Alkalinity (buffering), not pH alone
7. Which soil class is removed mainly by vacuuming and pile-lifting?
   Answer: A. Insoluble soil such as sand and grit
8. Which pair must never be mixed?
   Answer: D. An acid and chlorine

### Module 4: The Five Cleaning Principles

1. Which is Principle 3 of the five cleaning principles?
   Answer: C. Extraction of suspended soil
2. Why is Principle 1 never skipped?
   Answer: B. Skipping it grinds grit into the yarn during suspension
3. About how long should most pre-sprays dwell on the fibre?
   Answer: A. 5 to 10 minutes
4. In the dry-stroke technique the typical trigger-to-vacuum ratio is:
   Answer: D. 1:2, with half the stroke vacuum only
5. How much should wand strokes overlap?
   Answer: C. About 25 per cent
6. What is the CARSI residential drying target?
   Answer: B. Safe to walk on within 2 hours, fully dry within 12 hours
7. What is the commercial drying target?
   Answer: A. Back in service in 8 hours or less
8. When do you complete the top half of the pre-inspection form?
   Answer: D. Before any chemistry touches the fibre

### Module 5: Spot and Stain Removal

1. What is the difference between a spot and a stain?
   Answer: C. A stain has permanently altered the fibre's dye structure
2. Which stain class does coffee belong to?
   Answer: B. Tannin
3. What do you do first with a fresh spill?
   Answer: A. Blot it, never rub
4. You have treated a stain with appropriate chemistry three times and it has not visibly improved. What now?
   Answer: D. Stop and disclose honestly to the customer
5. Aged pet urine can reach what pH, and what do you try first?
   Answer: C. pH 10 to 12; an acid-side spotter first
6. Which combination must never be mixed?
   Answer: B. An acid spotter and chlorine bleach
7. Why does the oxidiser stay apart from the reducing agent?
   Answer: A. They cancel each other and can give off heat
8. When should you reinspect a treated stain?
   Answer: D. After 24 hours, because some stains wick back

### Module 6: Upholstery Theory: Fibre and Fabric Construction

1. Which construction type is known to distort, crush or watermark under moisture?
   Answer: C. Non-durable constructions such as velvet and chintz, or quilted fabrics
2. What does a cleaning code of X mean?
   Answer: B. Professional vacuuming only
3. A tag says W. What should you still do?
   Answer: A. Pre-test with the four tests
4. What does a code of S allow?
   Answer: D. Solvent-based cleaning only
5. What are the four pre-inspection tests?
   Answer: C. Bleed, crock, shrink, distortion
6. How large is the shrink-test patch, and when is it read?
   Answer: B. About 50 by 50 mm, read at 24 hours
7. How much strength can rayon lose when wet, according to the manual?
   Answer: A. Up to 70 per cent
8. If one of the four tests fails you should:
   Answer: D. Stop and reassess the method

### Module 7: Upholstery Hands-On

1. Which upholstery method is best for S-code fabric and oily soil?
   Answer: C. Dry solvent
2. Why is no live steam used on upholstery?
   Answer: B. It can damage adhesives and glues, and collapse latex foam
3. What CARSI jet temperature is recommended on olefin?
   Answer: A. 60 degrees Celsius
4. Which tool tier is the fastest and over-wets the least?
   Answer: D. Air-driven or external-jet tool
5. Where do you point the air when speed-drying upholstery?
   Answer: C. Across the fabric, not into it
6. What is the hand-dry target on sturdy W-code fabric?
   Answer: B. Within 2 hours
7. Which method leaves residue if not rinsed and is rarely a first choice today?
   Answer: A. Shampoo
8. Above what relative humidity should you open a window or run a dehumidifier on an upholstery job?
   Answer: D. 60 per cent

### Module 8: Hard Floors

1. Which of these is calcareous and etched by acid?
   Answer: C. Marble
2. What does fizzing with a drop of distilled white vinegar tell you?
   Answer: B. The stone is calcareous, so never use acid
3. A non-destructive inspection allows you to:
   Answer: A. Measure and observe, then document and photograph
4. What is the weak point of ceramic tile flooring?
   Answer: D. The grout
5. What happens to laminate joints that are saturated?
   Answer: C. They swell badly
6. Which cleaner starter suits marble?
   Answer: B. A neutral-pH stone-safe cleaner
7. What should every inspection photograph have?
   Answer: A. A date, a scale reference and a caption
8. What does the guide say about the supplier's hard-floor chemistry range in April 2026?
   Answer: D. It is carpet-and-upholstery focused, so ask the counter or a specialist supplier

### Module 9: Machinery and Preventive Maintenance

1. A Razorback Balboa 700 Auto ships with a single 10 amp cord. What cord rating do the larger TRACTX units ship with?
   Answer: C. 15 amp
2. At how many hours do you change the oil on a Kohler EFI truck-mount engine with the extended programme, and what is the trap?
   Answer: B. Every 300 hours; applying that interval to a non-EFI engine that needs changes at 50 to 100 hours
3. New vacuum motor brushes measure about 25 mm. At what length do you condemn them, and why replace before they fail?
   Answer: A. About 8 mm; a worn brush arcs, pits the windings and writes off a motor that costs ten to fifteen times as much
4. What household liquid, at about 1 L to 8 L of water, do you flush quarterly through a portable's solution system, and why?
   Answer: D. White vinegar, to remove alkaline and hard-water scale
5. A dual-heat portable still runs but jobs need more dwell each month. What silent fault do you suspect?
   Answer: C. A scaled heat exchanger coil, prevented by the quarterly vinegar flush
6. Which daily task most directly protects a portable's vacuum motors from water damage?
   Answer: B. Cleaning the vacuum inlet filter and checking the float ball moves freely
7. How long is the daily checklist meant to take before the first job?
   Answer: A. About 3 minutes

### Module 10: The Business of Cleaning

1. How many cost lines make up a job, and which one is what you take home?
   Answer: D. Six; profit margin
2. Which pricing model is the default for commercial work?
   Answer: C. Per square metre
3. What does a minimum call-out cover?

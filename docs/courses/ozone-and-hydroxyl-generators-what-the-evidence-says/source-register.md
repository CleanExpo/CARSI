## Research method

Primary public sources only. Each source was downloaded and the passages relied on were read in the
source's own text on 14/10/2026: regulator pages and PDFs were fetched directly and searched for the
exact passage; peer-reviewed papers were read as their PubMed records (citation and abstract, via the
NCBI E-utilities). No trade-press article, supplier blog or web-scraped copy of any standard was used
as a basis for a claim. Where only an abstract was read, the course claims nothing beyond the abstract.

IICRC standards: no standard text was fetched, read or entered into any AI tool. The course, quiz and
launch copy name no standard and make no claim, positive or absent, about what any standard contains.
Lesson 1 retells the July near miss without saying what the standard does or does not contain.

CARSI-recommended practice in Lesson 4 (clear the space, protect susceptible items, measure with a
calibrated meter, RCD-protected GPO, test-and-tag, job records, and "if you cannot show why the space
is ready, treat it as not ready") is labelled in the lesson as CARSI's recommendation, built from S1-S3,
S10 and S11, not as a legal requirement.

Occupant re-entry: the course gives **no** numeric re-entry threshold. The 0.1 ppm peak limit is taught
as a workplace exposure limit for workers (S1, S2) that protects most but not all people (S11). When
occupants return is taught as a site-specific decision that follows the manufacturer's instructions and
any applicable state or territory guidance and rests on measured readings, with the EPA's cautions that
levels above health standards are possible even when manufacturers' instructions are followed and that
little is known about treatment by-products (S3). No primary source read for this course states a
general occupant re-entry concentration, so none is stated. (Corrected 14/10/2026 after Codex review
codex-carsi-b7077ba9-20261014 finding P1-UNSUPPORTED-REENTRY-CLEARANCE: the earlier draft treated a
reading under the worker peak limit as re-entry clearance for anyone.)

## Claims dropped for lack of evidence

- Named products, models, Australian 240 V specifications and AUD prices: no manufacturer spec sheet
  for an Australian 240 V ozone or hydroxyl unit could be verified on 14/10/2026 (one US distributor
  page timed out; search results were US 120 V listings and trade press). No product is named.
- 50 Hz mains frequency: no primary government source was found in the time available; the lesson
  states only the AER-sourced 230 V figure.
- Hydroxyl generators being safe in occupied rooms, and hydroxyl removing smoke or sewage odour on
  real jobs: the sources read do not establish either. The course states this as a verification gap,
  not as a finding that the machines fail.
- NSW Health ozone generator factsheet: the address cited by a supplier page returns 404; not used.
- APVMA position on these devices: none specific was found on apvma.gov.au; nothing is stated.
- "5-10 times" is used only as the EPA's summary of earlier studies, not as an independent finding.
- Any statement about what an industry standard contains, positive or absent. CARSI policy requires a
  positive claim about a standard to cite a section that exists in the licensed index, and none was
  available, so the course and launch copy make none. (Removed 14/10/2026 after Codex review
  codex-carsi-b7077ba9-20261014 finding P1-STANDARDS-SECTION-POLICY.)
